# Research bibliography and applicability audit

Checked against primary publisher, author or proceedings sources on 7 October 2026. URL is supplied where a DOI is absent. Earlier preliminary notes have been audited against primary sources; this bibliography documents the primary literature and applicability boundaries. In particular, neither Howard nor Blackwell establishes the unrestricted zero-VoI claim, and no cited online matching ratio transfers automatically to this simulator.

## Dynamic markets, online allocation and stopping

1. **Akbarpour, Mohammad; Li, Shengwu; Oveis Gharan, Shayan (2020).** “Thickness and Information in Dynamic Matching Markets.” *Journal of Political Economy* 128(3), 783-815. DOI: [10.1086/704761](https://doi.org/10.1086/704761). [Author manuscript](https://web.stanford.edu/~mohamwad/DynamicMatching.pdf). Waiting and knowledge of imminent departures interact. The present simulator does not reveal imminent exits; its finite market and repeat introductions differ from this asymptotic model.
2. **Baccara, Mariagiovanna; Lee, SangMok; Yariv, Leeat (2020).** “Optimal Dynamic Matching.” *Theoretical Economics* 15(3), 1221-1278. DOI: [10.3982/TE3740](https://doi.org/10.3982/TE3740). [Author manuscript](https://www.lyariv.com/papers/DynamicMatching.pdf). Optimal waiting depends on joint surplus and market composition; a scalar time threshold is a restriction, not the general Bellman solution.
3. **Karp, Richard M.; Vazirani, Umesh V.; Vazirani, Vijay V. (1990).** “An Optimal Algorithm for On-line Bipartite Matching.” *STOC*, 352-358. DOI: [10.1145/100216.100262](https://doi.org/10.1145/100216.100262). [Berkeley technical report version](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/1991/1708.html). Ranking's 1-1/e result concerns irrevocable one-sided arrivals and unweighted bipartite matching, not repeat dyads with delayed retirement.
4. **Mehta, Aranyak; Saberi, Amin; Vazirani, Umesh; Vazirani, Vijay (2007).** “AdWords and Generalized Online Matching.” *Journal of the ACM* 54(5), Article 22. DOI: [10.1145/1284320.1284321](https://doi.org/10.1145/1284320.1284321). [Author paper](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~vazirani/pubs/adwords.pdf). Budget-sensitive LP dual prices motivate opportunity costs; the allocation model and small-bid assumptions must be checked before transferring guarantees.
5. **Manshadi, Vahideh H.; Oveis Gharan, Shayan; Saberi, Amin (2012).** “Online Stochastic Matching: Online Actions Based on Offline Statistics.” *Mathematics of Operations Research* 37(4), 559-573. DOI: [10.1287/moor.1120.0551](https://doi.org/10.1287/moor.1120.0551). Stochastic arrival distributions enable improved matching; its bipartite bins-and-balls setting does not establish a guarantee here.
6. **Huang, Zhiyi; Kang, Ning; Tang, Zhihao Gavin; Wu, Xiaowei; Zhang, Yuhao; Zhu, Xue (2020).** “Fully Online Matching.” *Journal of the ACM* 67(3), Article 18. DOI: [10.1145/3390890](https://doi.org/10.1145/3390890). Both sides arrive online; deadlines and general graphs change competitive bounds. The simulator allows reuse after unsuccessful introductions and has no action for cancelling a busy pair.
7. **Huang, Zhiyi; Tang, Zhihao Gavin; Wu, Xiaowei; Zhang, Yuhao (2020).** “Fully Online Matching II: Beating Ranking and Water-filling.” *FOCS*. [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.06311). Balanced Ranking and Eager Water-filling illustrate distinct integral/fractional general-graph guarantees. They are not interchangeable with stochastic MSMI guarantees.
8. **Whittle, Peter (1988).** “Restless Bandits: Activity Allocation in a Changing World.” *Journal of Applied Probability* 25(A), 287-298. DOI: [10.2307/3214163](https://doi.org/10.2307/3214163). Relaxing an expected activation constraint motivates an index. Overlapping dyads violate independent-project transitions; indexability is an additional property to prove.
9. **Gittins, John C. (1979).** “Bandit Processes and Dynamic Allocation Indices.” *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society, Series B* 41(2), 148-177 (including discussion). [Publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1979.tb01068.x). Independent rested arms are essential to the classical index reduction; simultaneous dyadic capacity consumption lacks that structure.
10. **Gilbert, John P.; Mosteller, Frederick (1966).** “Recognizing the Maximum of a Sequence.” *Journal of the American Statistical Association* 61(313), 35-73. DOI: [10.1080/01621459.1966.10502008](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1966.10502008). Full-information best-choice and expected-utility stopping objectives must be distinguished; the simulator maximizes repeated terminal rewards, not the probability of selecting the single best offer.

## Reciprocal recommendation

11. **Pizzato, Luiz; Rej, Tomek; Chung, Thomas; Koprinska, Irena; Kay, Judy (2010).** “RECON: A Reciprocal Recommender for Online Dating.” *ACM RecSys*, 207-214. [Paper](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=1eba75ecbb4878d419298fb77f585b6e8955e8a4&repid=rep1&type=pdf). Both evaluator and candidate preferences matter. Reciprocal aggregation is a ranking design; it does not prove harmonic fusion is a joint Bernoulli probability.
12. **Xia, Peng; Liu, Benyuan; Sun, Yizhou; Chen, Cindy (2015).** “Reciprocal Recommendation System for Online Dating.” *IEEE/ACM ASONAM*. [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.06247). Reciprocity-aware recommendations outperform one-sided formulations in their dataset; this is motivation rather than a transferable performance theorem.
13. **Hitsch, Günter J.; Hortaçsu, Ali; Ariely, Dan (2010).** “Matching and Sorting in Online Dating.” *American Economic Review* 100(1), 130-163. DOI: [10.1257/aer.100.1.130](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.100.1.130). Observed choices and feasible mutual matches are distinct. Real dating findings do not validate invented simulator coefficients.

## Delay, survival and censoring

14. **Chapelle, Olivier (2014).** “Modeling Delayed Feedback in Display Advertising.” *ACM KDD*, 1097-1105. DOI: [10.1145/2623330.2623634](https://doi.org/10.1145/2623330.2623634). [Paper](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=9eed333dcaade2c1dd32590eff6b0c0e670a5674&repid=rep1&type=pdf). A pending negative label mixes eventual conversion and nonconversion; an explicit delay model avoids treating it as an observed failure.
15. **Vernade, Claire; Cappé, Olivier; Perchet, Vianney (2017).** “Stochastic Bandit Models for Delayed Conversions.” *UAI*. [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.09186). Known delay distributions and censoring determine the effective number of observed trials. The bibliography previously assigned this UAI paper to PMLR 73; that attribution has been removed.
16. **Joulani, Pooria; György, András; Szepesvári, Csaba (2013).** “Online Learning under Delayed Feedback.” *ICML*, PMLR 28(3), 1453-1461. [Proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v28/joulani13.html). Delay effects differ between adversarial and stochastic settings; their reductions do not establish a specific regret constant for this changing matching graph.
17. **Kaplan, Edward L.; Meier, Paul (1958).** “Nonparametric Estimation from Incomplete Observations.” *Journal of the American Statistical Association* 53(282), 457-481. DOI: [10.1080/01621459.1958.10501452](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1958.10501452). [Paper](https://jhanley.biostat.mcgill.ca/bios601/SurvivalAnalysis/KaplanMeier1958.pdf). Product-limit estimation handles administrative censoring; permanent no-response is a competing outcome, not harmless censoring.

## Graph optimization and combinatorial learning

18. **Edmonds, Jack (1965).** “Paths, Trees, and Flowers.” *Canadian Journal of Mathematics* 17, 449-467. DOI: [10.4153/CJM-1965-045-4](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1965-045-4). Odd cycles require general-graph matching. This foundational maximum-cardinality paper should not alone be cited as the complete modern weighted implementation.
19. **Galil, Zvi (1986).** “Efficient Algorithms for Finding Maximum Matching in Graphs.” *ACM Computing Surveys* 18(1), 23-38. DOI: [10.1145/6462.6502](https://doi.org/10.1145/6462.6502). Weighted primal-dual blossom algorithms underlie the implementation used here.
20. **Chen, Wei; Wang, Yajun; Yuan, Yang (2013).** “Combinatorial Multi-Armed Bandit: General Framework and Applications.” *ICML*, PMLR 28(1), 151-159. [Proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v28/chen13a.html). Oracle-based combinatorial exploration needs appropriate reward regularity and arm assumptions; changing availability and common dyadic effects require additional analysis.
21. **NetworkX developers (2026, version 3.7).** “max_weight_matching.” [Official documentation](https://networkx.org/documentation/stable/reference/algorithms/generated/networkx.algorithms.matching.max_weight_matching.html). O(|V|³) implementation; floating-point weights can introduce small numerical suboptimality. Runtime interruption and legal greedy fallback are policy engineering additions, not library guarantees.

## Information elicitation and drift

22. **Howard, Ronald A. (1966).** “Information Value Theory.” *IEEE Transactions on Systems Science and Cybernetics* 2(1), 22-26. [Publisher paper](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/4082064/). Information value depends on whether observations can change an optimal action; no unconditional hard-first theorem is asserted by this source.
23. **Blackwell, David (1953).** “Equivalent Comparisons of Experiments.” *Annals of Mathematical Statistics* 24(2), 265-272. DOI: [10.1214/aoms/1177729032](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177729032). [Publisher](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177729032). More informative experiments weakly improve optimal expected decisions. Equality requires additional decision-invariance conditions.
24. **Krause, Andreas; Guestrin, Carlos (2005).** “Near-optimal Nonmyopic Value of Information in Graphical Models.” *UAI*, 324-331. [Author paper](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~guestrin/Publications/UAI2005/uai2005.pdf). Submodular information objectives admit approximation results under their assumptions; feasible-edge creation here has query complementarity and need not be submodular.
25. **Gama, João; Žliobaitė, Indrė; Bifet, Albert; Pechenizkiy, Mykola; Bouchachia, Abdelhamid (2014).** “A Survey on Concept Drift Adaptation.” *ACM Computing Surveys* 46(4), Article 44, 1-37. DOI: [10.1145/2523813](https://doi.org/10.1145/2523813). [Institutional record](https://researchportal.ip-paris.fr/en/publications/a-survey-on-concept-drift-adaptation/). Distinguish parameter drift, feature distribution change and missing-label processes before applying a detector.
26. **Garivier, Aurélien; Moulines, Eric (2011).** “On Upper-Confidence Bound Policies for Non-Stationary Bandit Problems.” *ALT*, LNCS 6925, 174-188. [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/0805.3415). Sliding-window and discounted estimates adapt to changes at an information cost; tiny per-episode samples limit reliable sign detection.
27. **Russac, Yoan; Cappé, Olivier; Garivier, Aurélien (2020).** “Algorithms for Non-Stationary Generalized Linear Bandits.” *NeurIPS*. [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.10113). Windowed/discounted GLM confidence methods motivate further adaptation; the present diagonal approximation is not that algorithm and inherits none of its confidence guarantees.

## Verification scope

Primary sources were checked for the central claims (dynamic matching, online allocation, Whittle, delayed feedback, Blackwell/Howard, combinatorial learning, drift and NetworkX). Related citations provide traceable publisher/author links; this is not a claim that every proof in every cited paper was independently replicated. Literature results are attributed locally in REPORT.md; simulator-specific results come from executed code. The phrase “Howard-Blackwell Invariance Principle” is not treated as an established named theorem.
